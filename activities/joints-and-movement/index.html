Meet the Five Joint Types

Learn what kind of movement each joint allows.

1

Ball-and-Socket

Allows movement in many directions. Example: shoulder and hip.

2

Hinge

Mainly allows bending and straightening. Example: elbow and knee.

3

Pivot

Allows one part to rotate around another. Example: neck.

4

↔ Gliding

Allows bones to slide or glide over each other. Example: some wrist and ankle joints.

5

Fixed

Does not allow noticeable movement. Example: joints between skull bones.

Classroom Guide

Discussion Prompt: Ask students: “What happens when you bend your elbow? Can your elbow rotate in every direction?”
Prediction Prompt: Ask students to predict the joint type before revealing the answer.
Demonstration: Ask students to slowly move their head, arm and wrist and describe the movement.
Group Activity: Divide students into groups. Give each group a body movement and ask them to identify the joint type.
Think & Explain: Ask: “Why would a fixed joint be useful in the skull?”

Quick Teaching Reference

Ball-and-Socket

Shoulder and hip. Allows movement in several directions.

Hinge

Elbow and knee. Mainly allows bending and straightening.

Pivot

Neck. Allows rotational movement.

Gliding

Some wrist and ankle joints allow bones to glide over one another.

Fixed

Skull bones have fixed joints that provide stability and protection.
